TR-909: an older machine in a newer club

Follow one row of lights. Listen for what changes around it.

The object’s story

Roland introduced the TR-909 Rhythm Composer in 1983. Its importance to 1990s house and techno comes from continued use of an earlier instrument. The machine combines analogue drum synthesis with sampled hi-hats and cymbals, plus a sequencer and MIDI connections. This is the original model, not Roland’s much later miniature TR-09.

Build a pattern, then alter it

Sixteen step buttons make a pattern visible. Individual sound controls shape the drum voices, while timing functions such as shuffle and flam change how a sequence feels. Programming repeated hits is only the starting point: sound, spacing and interaction with the rest of a track determine whether a loop moves. It is a rhythm instrument, not a sampler that records an arbitrary piece of another song.

An older design finds a new setting

Designer Tadao Kikumoto recalls learning of renewed interest in the early 1990s. Alan Braxe gives a concrete later example: TR-909 drums sit beside a Chaka Khan sample and Korg Trident bass in Stardust’s 1998 Music Sounds Better With You. Follow the French-house guide to hear those different jobs. The reference photograph shows this model’s controls; the unit is not identified as the one from that session.
