Before one phone did everything

A pager could tell you somebody wanted a response. A PalmPilot could carry a calendar and contacts. A mobile handset could move a call away from the household line. These were different jobs with different controls, connections, and limits, rather than three interchangeable versions of the same future device.

The important question was what could leave the desk. An organizer still worked with a desktop copy of information; a pager could require finding another telephone to reply; a handset depended on its service and available power. Their overlapping uses explain why the pocket could become crowded before any one device seemed able to replace the others.

The family computer becomes a portal

About this object

Someone needs the phone line. Finish the download before you give up the chair.

A desktop PC makes a fixed place in the home into several different workplaces. The Rotterdam setup photographed in 1999 puts a CRT monitor, keyboard and separate case at one desk. Writing, playing a game and going online use the same chair, even when the people doing them have different ideas about whose turn comes next.

Alphanumeric pager

About this object

The pager goes off. Finding a phone is your next problem.

The TELTRIM-branded alphanumeric pager in the reference photograph receives short messages on a compact display. Its visible badge does not establish a Motorola model. An alert can reach the wearer away from a desk without supplying the full two-way conversation of a telephone.
