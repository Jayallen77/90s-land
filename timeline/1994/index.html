THE DECADE, DAY BY DAY

1994

The coffee shop and the loading screen

Friends and ER joined the weekly television routine. Dookie grew into a hit, Brazil won a penalty shootout, and Japan got its first PlayStation. South Africa held its first democratic election; Britain and France opened a tunnel.

one person's corner of the web · 1994–1998

Handmade GeoCities homepage

About this object

Tiled stars, a loud title, and an under-construction badge made the page feel visibly owned.

This exhibit is an original HTML/CSS recreation by 90s.land. It illustrates the personal-homepage vocabulary associated with the early web: a title, strong background choices, a collection of interests, and visible signs that the page is still being made. It is not a recovered GeoCities page or an account belonging to a historical user.

the console grows up · 1994–1999

Original PlayStation hardware

About this object

Put the disc in. Keep the memory card somewhere you can find it.

Sony’s original PlayStation has a top-loading disc lid, two controller ports, and separate slots for memory cards. These openings reveal different jobs: the disc supplies the game, the controller supplies the player’s actions, and the card can preserve progress after the machine is switched off.

Evan-Amos ↗ · Public domain

shared-screen gravity · 1990–1999

Living-room CRT television

About this object

The episode starts at eight. Get the good spot on the sofa.

A cathode-ray-tube television made the screen part of a substantial cabinet. The curved glass and the depth behind it affected where the set could stand, how it was moved, and how furniture gathered around it. A television was a location inside the room as well as an image.
