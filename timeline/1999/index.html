1999

One more disc before midnight

Britney’s debut album and The Sopranos opened the year. The USA won the Women’s World Cup, Sega chose 9/9/99 for its American Dreamcast launch, and response teams worked through the millennium countdown.

a modem inside the console · 1999

Sega Dreamcast

About this object

The little VMU had a screen of its own. Keep it with the controller.

Sega’s Dreamcast places optical-disc games beside a controller with accessory slots. The pictured VMU adds a small screen to saved-data storage and sits visibly in the controller. A memory accessory becomes part of what a player can look at while holding the controls.

the countdown becomes infrastructure · 1999

Y2K response office

About this object

Rows of screens and response teams turned a date change into nightly-news suspense.

Japan’s Year 2000 response office, photographed with Prime Minister Keizō Obuchi on December 31, 1999, shows people organized around monitoring and coordination. The setting makes an information-system problem visible as a room, a timetable and assigned responsibility.

Cabinet Public Affairs Office, Government of Japan / Prime Minister’s Office website ↗ · CC BY 4.0 (Government of Japan Standard Terms of Use 2.0)

shared-room portal · 1995–1999

The family computer becomes a portal

About this object

Someone needs the phone line. Finish the download before you give up the chair.

A desktop PC makes a fixed place in the home into several different workplaces. The Rotterdam setup photographed in 1999 puts a CRT monitor, keyboard and separate case at one desk. Writing, playing a game and going online use the same chair, even when the people doing them have different ideas about whose turn comes next.

Oompje ↗ · CC BY-SA 4.0

burn-your-own future · 1997–1999

Blank CD-R spindle

About this object

The rainbow discs waited to become a summer mix, a backup, or a folder of badly labeled MP3s.

A spindle of CD-Rs is a supply of blank recordable discs. A compatible writer and appropriate software can turn one into an audio compilation or a data disc. Those are different formats even when their finished objects look almost identical.
