THE DECADE, DAY BY DAY

1999

One more disc before midnight

Britney’s debut album and The Sopranos opened the year. The USA won the Women’s World Cup, Sega chose 9/9/99 for its American Dreamcast launch, and response teams worked through the millennium countdown.

Defining moments ↓Full calendar ↓Objects ↓Stories ↓

Start here for a taste of 1999, then follow any date into its sources and connections.

  1. Jan 1, 1999 · World news

    The euro begins without notes or coins

    Eleven countries enter monetary union; physical euro cash comes later.

  2. Jan 10, 1999 · Television

    The Sopranos starts on HBO

    A New Jersey mob boss brings his problems into a therapist’s office.

  3. Jan 12, 1999 · Music

    Britney Spears releases her debut album

    …Baby One More Time moves from a hit single into an album.

  4. Jan 21, 1999 · Games

    Super Smash Bros. puts Nintendo’s cast together

    Mario and Link can finally settle an argument on the same screen.

  5. Mar 31, 1999 · Movies

  6. Jul 10, 1999 · Sports

    The USA wins the Women’s World Cup

    Brandi Chastain’s penalty ends a tense final against China.

  7. Jul 14, 1999 · Movies

    The Blair Witch Project reaches theaters

    The July opening brings the woods into the summer cinema schedule.

  8. Sep 9, 1999 · Games

    Dreamcast’s 9/9/99 U.S. launch

    SEGA’s new console arrives in the United States.

  9. Nov 21, 1999 · Games

    Pokémon Gold and Silver open a new journey

    The Game Boy series returns with two new Japanese editions.

  10. Dec 31, 1999 · Technology

    Response teams watch the Y2K rollover

a modem inside the console · 1999

Sega Dreamcast

About this object

The little VMU had a screen of its own. Keep it with the controller.

Dreamcast paired its console and controller with a removable Visual Memory Unit. Its US launch date, 9/9/99, was easy to put on a calendar.

Y2K response office

About this object

Rows of screens and response teams turned a date change into nightly-news suspense.

Y2K made invisible software dependencies feel like a public cultural object.
