## The meter beside the screen

A connection was something a household opened and closed. There was a modem to dial, a telephone line to occupy and, depending on the service plan, a clock worth watching. A person could be deep in a conversation while another person was thinking about the bill. Getting online involved negotiating access to the machine and deciding how long to stay.

That arrangement gave the internet a visible edge. It was possible to finish a session, disconnect and leave the chair. Messages and pages might still exist elsewhere, but the household’s doorway to them was closed. ## An announcement is not an activation date

On October 29, 1996, AOL announced a flat monthly plan of $19.95. A contemporary account in The Motley Fool specifies the December billing cycle. The difference was practical. An hourly meter encourages a person to prepare before dialing and finish quickly. A flat plan changes the value of another minute. ## Unlimited time still met a busy line

The new plan did not remove the household’s physical constraints. A modem still needed a connection. A shared computer still had a chair. Another person might still need to call someone.
