A recording tucked inside a protective shell. · 1992–1999 · era of use

Sony MZ-1 and a MiniDisc

About this object

A recording tucked inside a protective shell.

MiniDisc offered a different relationship with a personal music collection. Recording and organizing tracks could happen on a dedicated machine, without turning every listening session into a computer session. It was neither a cassette with a nicer shell nor simply a shrunken CD. Open the related story for the practical appeal of editing a portable recording, and the limits of a format that coexisted with several other ways to carry music.

Progress became something you could carry. · 1994–1999 · era of use

PlayStation memory card

About this object

Progress became something you could carry.

A borrowed game and a borrowed save were different transactions. The card could preserve a season, a campaign or the point before a difficult encounter, then go to another console in a bag. Space had to be managed, and deleting a save could have consequences far beyond clearing a menu. Put the card next to the controller and demo-disc feature: together they show a machine becoming a personal collection rather than an interchangeable player for discs.

Evan-Amos ↗ · Public domain

A photograph you could inspect immediately. · 1995–1999 · era of use

Casio QV-10

About this object

A photograph you could inspect immediately.

Immediate review changed the relationship between the shutter and the finished result. A disappointing frame could become information for the next attempt before the scene disappeared. The digital image still needed a route to a computer or another display, so the camera did not make every photographic task effortless. Place it beside QuickSnap to explore two systems that overlapped in the decade: one built around a developing interval, the other around inspecting a picture on the spot.

Morio ↗ · CC BY-SA 3.0

Playback made a voice into a toy. · 1993–1999 · era of use

Deluxe Talkboy recorder

About this object

Playback made a voice into a toy.

Recording a sentence and changing its playback made an ordinary voice strange enough to invite another try. The toy combined a familiar cassette medium with permission to experiment. It belongs in the same room as an answering machine, although the intentions were different: one preserved a message, the other made playing with the message the point. Follow the toy story for the difference between objects that waited for a command and objects that seemed to answer back.
